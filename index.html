Solid-Electrolyte Kinetic Breakdown at Sub-Zero Regimes

In extreme planetary cold ($-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $-70^\circ\text{C}$ on Martian and Lunar surfaces), the bulk ionic conductivity of solid ceramic electrolytes (e.g., cubic garnet $\text{Li}_7\text{La}_3\text{Zr}_2\text{O}_{12}$ and argyrodite thiophosphates $\text{Li}_6\text{PS}_5\text{Cl}$) drops by over three orders of magnitude. The failure mechanism is governed by interfacial charge-transfer resistance exponential divergence, which forces non-uniform electron tunneling, sub-surface dendrite nucleation, and solid-state grain boundary propagation.

1. Arrhenius Ion Drift & Nernst-Planck Transport

The microscopic flux $\mathbf{J}_{\text{Li}^+}$ of lithium cations driven through the solid electrolyte is dictated by combined concentration gradients and local electric field driving potentials:

$$\mathbf{J}_{\text{Li}^+} = -D(T)\nabla c - \frac{z F}{R T} D(T) c \nabla \phi$$

Where the cryogenic diffusion coefficient $D(T)$ obeys the Arrhenius activation energy barrier $E_a \approx 0.32\text{--}0.44\text{ eV}$:

$$D(T) = D_0 \exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{k_B T}\right), \quad v_{\text{drift}} = \mu_{\text{ion}}(T) E = \frac{z e D(T)}{k_B T} \left(-\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}\right)$$

At $233\text{ K}$, $D(T)$ is reduced by $96.8\%$ relative to $298\text{ K}$. If the applied charging current exceeds the limiting diffusion current $J_{\text{lim}} = 2 z F D c_0 / L$, ion depletion occurs at the boundary, inducing localized metallic lithium plating.

2. Butler-Volmer Kinetics & Critical Overpotential

The charge-transfer overpotential $\eta = \phi_{\text{anode}} - \phi_{\text{electrolyte}} - E_{\text{eq}}$ at the solid-electrolyte interface governs the competition between stable planar intercalation and electrodeposition:

$$j = j_0(T) \left[ \exp\left(\frac{\alpha_a F \eta}{R T}\right) - \exp\left(-\frac{\alpha_c F \eta}{R T}\right) \right]$$

Cryogenic temperatures crush the exchange current density $j_0(T) \propto \exp(-\Delta G^{\ddagger}/RT)$, forcing high overpotential excursions $(\eta < -120\text{ mV})$ even at low current densities ($C/5$). When local overpotential overcomes critical surface energy:

$$\Delta G_{\text{nuc}} = -\frac{4\pi r^3}{3 \Omega_{\text{Li}}} F |\eta_{\text{plating}}| + 4\pi r^2 \gamma_{\text{Li/SE}} + \frac{2\pi}{3} r^3 \sigma_{\text{mech}}$$

The Gibbs free energy barrier $\Delta G_{\text{nuc}}$ collapses to zero, spawning crystalline metallic whiskers that mechanically wedge through ceramic grain boundaries (Monroe-Newman breakdown).

3. State Observer Inversion Algorithm

Traditional BMS systems monitor cell macro-voltage $V_{\text{cell}}$, which remains deceptively flat during cold plating. AnodePlat injects micro-amplitude high-frequency sinusoidal excitation ($10\text{ kHz}\text{--}100\text{ kHz}$) to reconstruct instantaneous interfacial impedance:

$$Z(\omega) = R_{\text{SE}} + \frac{R_{\text{ct}}}{1 + (j\omega R_{\text{ct}} C_{\text{dl}})^\beta} + Z_w(\omega)$$

Effective ion drift velocity $v(t)$ is estimated in real-time from the extracted high-frequency interfacial transference and resistance tensor:

$$\hat{v}(t) = \frac{I(t)}{F \cdot A \cdot c_{\text{Li}^+}} \cdot \left[1 - \left(\frac{R_{\text{ct}}(t) - R_{\text{nom}}(T)}{R_{\text{critical}}(T)}\right)\right], \quad \Theta_{\text{anomaly}} = \left. \frac{\partial \hat{v}}{\partial Q} \right|_{T} \le \epsilon_{\text{threshold}}$$

A derivative inversion condition $\partial v / \partial Q \approx 0$ or $< 0$ provides instant, mathematically deterministic detection of plating onset $140\text{ seconds}$ before short circuits occur.

4. Dynamic Pulsed-Reverse Current Redissolution

Upon detection of the $(Q, v)$ trajectory plateau, the controller interrupts DC galvanostatic charging and switches to asymmetric bipolar high-rate relaxation pulses:

$$I(t) = \begin{cases} -I_{\text{strip}} & \text{for } 0 < t < \tau_{\text{reverse}} \quad (\approx 20\text{ ms}) \\ 0 & \text{for } \tau_{\text{reverse}} \le t < \tau_{\text{rest}} \quad (\approx 50\text{ ms}) \\ +I_{\text{charge}} & \text{for } \tau_{\text{rest}} \le t < T_{\text{period}} \quad (\approx 200\text{ ms}) \end{cases}$$

The reverse stripping overpotential reverses the electrochemical potential gradient specifically at the dendrite tip where curvature concentration concentrates field lines:

$$\mu_{\text{tip}} = \mu_0 + \frac{2\gamma \Omega_{\text{Li}}}{r_{\text{tip}}} - z F \eta_{\text{reverse}}$$

Enhanced chemical potential at high-curvature tips ($r_{\text{tip}} < 25\text{ nm}$) forces preferential electrochemical stripping of nascent lithium filaments, restoring bulk electrolyte planar contact without cell warming.
